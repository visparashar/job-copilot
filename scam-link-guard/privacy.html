Scam Link Guard — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026 (version 1.2)

Summary

Scam Link Guard checks links on your own computer. It does not collect, send, sell or share what you read, the links you see, or anything about you.

What the extension reads

On the supported chat, email and social-media websites, the extension reads the web addresses of links on the page and a short piece of the message around each link, so it can decide whether the link looks like a scam. This happens entirely inside your browser. The text is used only for that check and is not stored or sent anywhere.

On the supported chat and email websites it also reads the text of incoming messages to spot common scam messages (for example requests for an OTP, “digital arrest” threats or fake job offers), and shows a warning under the message. This also happens entirely inside your browser.

When you paste text into the “Check a message or link” box, or use the right-click “Check” options, that text is checked inside your browser and is not stored or sent anywhere.

QR codes

If QR checks are on, the extension reads QR codes in images shown in your chats, in images you paste or upload into the checker, and in an image you right-click and choose “Check this QR code”. When a QR code cannot be read directly from the image, the extension takes a one-time capture of the visible tab to read it. The QR code is decoded entirely inside your browser; the image and the capture are never stored or sent anywhere.

Optional: protection on every website

Only if you switch on “Protect me on every website” (and approve Chrome's permission prompt), the extension also checks the links on the other websites you visit, and the pages themselves: their address, title, headings and the labels of form fields (for example whether a page asks for a password, OTP or card number). It never reads what you type into those fields. This check also happens entirely inside your browser, and nothing is sent anywhere. You can switch it off at any time, which also removes the permission.

Optional: website age check

Scam websites are usually only days old. Only if you switch on “Check how new a suspicious website is”, when a link already looks suspicious the extension looks up the website's registration date in the public RDAP registry (through rdap.org, which forwards the question to the official registry for that domain). That request contains only the website's domain name (for example example.com) — never the full link, the page, the message, or anything about you. Results are kept on your device for 30 days so the same domain is not looked up again.

What it stores on your computer

  • Your settings (on/off, language and country) and the sites you chose to trust.
  • Simple counters (links checked, warnings shown, clicks stopped) and a list of the last 25 warnings (the web address, or the type of scam message — never the message text), shown in the extension's popup.
  • A public list of known phishing domains, and the extension's own list of scam rules (see below).
  • If the website age check is on: the registration dates of the domains it looked up.

All of this is kept in the browser's extension storage on your device. The developer has no access to it.

Network use

Once a day the extension downloads the public, open-source Phishing.Database list of known phishing domains from GitHub. This is a plain download of the whole list: no information about you, your browsing or the links you see is included in the request. Links are never sent to any server to be checked.

Once a day it also downloads a small rules file published by the developer on GitHub Pages (visparashar.github.io). It contains only data — names of organisations scammers imitate, their official websites, known scam websites and scam phrases — so new scams can be recognised without waiting for an extension update. It never contains code, and no information about you is sent to get it.

The optional website age check described above is the only time a domain name leaves your computer, and only when you switch it on.

What the extension does not do

  • No accounts, analytics, advertising, tracking or remote code.
  • No sale or transfer of data to anyone.
  • No use of data for anything other than warning you about scam links.

Sharing a warning

The “Warn family on WhatsApp” button opens WhatsApp's share page with a short warning text that you can review and send yourself. The scam link in it is written in a broken form so it cannot be clicked. Nothing is sent unless you send it. “Copy warning” only copies that text to your clipboard.

Donations

The “Buy me a coffee” button opens the developer's donation page in a new tab. Any payment happens on that third-party site under its own privacy policy.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the extension removes everything it stored.
